# Reference verification and priority assessment

This is a source audit for the later rewrite, not an assertion of novelty.
Metadata verification and reading an abstract are distinguished from comparing
the full equations. Access limitations are not evidence that a paper is absent.

## Corrections to the external review

1. **Whitman 1982 exists.** Patrick G. Whitman, *The Tolman-Wyman equation in
   general relativity*, Physics Letters A **89**, 129–130 (1982),
   [DOI 10.1016/0375-9601(82)90873-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/0375-9601(82)90873-8).
   Publisher metadata and indexed abstract verified. The abstract specifically
   connects the dragging equation to the homogeneous part of the Tolman–Wyman
   equation. Full text was not accessible in this pass. This is a close and
   important predecessor, not a suspect fabricated reference.

2. **Whitman 1983 must also be compared.** Patrick G. Whitman, *Solutions to the
   general-relativistic Tolman-Wyman equation*, Physical Review D **27**, 1722
   (1983), [DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.27.1722](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.27.1722).
   Publisher abstract describes generating new interiors using known particular
   integrals. Full article access requires a subscription on the observed page.
   The Delgaty–Lake catalogue lists Whitman II–IV and identifies this paper as
   reference 51. Full equation-by-equation comparison remains necessary.

3. **The review's Lake link is misattributed.**
   [gr-qc/0306109](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0306109) is Damien Martin and Matt
   Visser, *Algorithmic construction of static perfect fluid spheres*, Physical
   Review D **69**, 104028 (2004), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.69.104028.
   Lake's paper is Kayll Lake, *All static spherically symmetric perfect fluid
   solutions of Einstein's Equations*, Physical Review D **67**, 104015 (2003),
   [gr-qc/0209104](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0209104),
   DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.67.104015. Both should be cited for the relevant algorithms.

4. **Reina's publication details were correct.** Borja Reina, *Slowly rotating
   homogeneous masses revisited*, MNRAS **455**, 4512–4517 (2016),
   [DOI 10.1093/mnras/stv2599](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stv2599),
   [arXiv:1503.07835](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.07835).
   Author and publisher bibliographic record verified.

5. **The review's Fodor title does not match its link.**
   [gr-qc/0612080](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0612080) is Gyula Fodor,
   *Slowly rotating fluid balls with linear equation of state*, rather than the
   title quoted in the review. Use the verified title if citing that identifier.

## Priority assessment

The static generating framework, the connection between the dragging and
isotropy operators, and solving Hartle's second-order equations cannot be claimed
as new on the evidence available. The proposed distinctive contribution is the
specific polynomial choice enforcing the n=3/2 gas surface, together with its
fully characterized family and exact first-order moment of inertia.

Targeted searches and the inspected catalogue entries did not establish an
identical published polynomial family. That is a limited negative search result,
not proof of priority. Before a novelty claim, compare the Whitman papers in
full, check the metric/EOS invariants rather than notation alone, and inspect
the relevant book chapters and later generating papers. No paywall was bypassed.

No claim about acceptance by GRG, EPJC, CQG, or another journal is justified by
this audit. The next draft should make the contribution assessable without
predicting publication outcomes.
